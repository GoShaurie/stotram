[p. 869,2] ra/sa : m. (ifc. f(ā). ) the sap or juice of plants, juice of fruit, any liquid or fluid, the best or finest or prime part of anything, essence, marrow, RV. &c. &c.

[p. 869,2] : water, liquor, drink, MBh.; Kāv. &c.

[p. 869,2] : juice of the sugarcane, syrup, Suśr.

[p. 869,2] : any mixture, draught, elixir, potion, R.; BhP.

[p. 869,2] : melted butter, L.

[p. 869,2] : (with or scil. gavām) milk, MBh.

[p. 869,2] : (with or scil. viṣasya) poison, Daś.; Rājat.

[p. 869,3] : nectar, L.

[p. 869,3] : soup, broth, L.

[p. 869,3] : a constituent fluid or essential juice of the body, serum, (esp.) the primary juice called chyle (formed from the food and changed by the bile into blood), ib.

[p. 869,3] : mercury, quicksilver (sometimes regarded as a kind of quintessence of the human body, else where as the seminal fluid of Śiva), Sarvad.

[p. 869,3] : semen virile, RV. i, 105, 2

[p. 869,3] : myrrh, L.

[p. 869,3] : any mineral or metallic salt, Cat.

[p. 869,3] : a metal or mineral in a state of fusion (cf. upa-, mahā-r˚)

[p. 869,3] : gold, L.

[p. 869,3] : Vangueria Spinosa, L.

[p. 869,3] : a species of amaranth, L.

[p. 869,3] : green onion, L.

[p. 869,3] : resin, L.

[p. 869,3] : = amṛta, L.

[p. 869,3] : taste, flavour (as the principal quality of fluids, of which there are 6 original kinds, viz. madhura, sweet; amla, sour; lavaṇa, salt; kaṭuka, pungent; tikta, bitter; and kaṣāya, astringent; sometimes 63 varieties are distinguished, viz. beside the 6 original ones, 15 mixtures of 2, 20 of 3, 15 of 4, 6 of 5, and 1 of 6 flavours), ŚBr. &c. &c.

[p. 869,3] : N. of the number ‘six’, VarBṛS.; Śrutab.

[p. 869,3] : any object of taste, condiment, sauce, spice, seasoning, MBh.; Kāv. &c.

[p. 869,3] : the tongue (as the organ of taste), BhP.

[p. 869,3] : taste or inclination or fondness for (loc. with or scil. upari, or comp.), love, affection, desire, MBh.; Kāv. &c.

[p. 869,3] : charm, pleasure, delight, ib.

[p. 869,3] : (in rhet.) the taste or character of a work, the feeling or sentiment prevailing in it (from 8 to 10 Rasas are generally enumerated, viz. śṛṅgāra, love; vīra, heroism; bībhatsa, disgust; raudra, anger or fury; hāsya, mirth; bhayānaka, terror; karuṇa, pity; adbhuta, wonder; śānta, tranquillity or contentment; vātsalya, paternal fondness; the last or last two are sometimes omitted; cf. under bhāva), Bhar.; Daśar.; Kāvyād. &c.

[p. 869,3] : the prevailing sentiment in human character, Uttarar.; Rājat.

[p. 869,3] : (with Vaiṣṇavas) disposition of the heart or mind, religious sentiment (there are 5 Rasas or Ratis forming the 5 degrees of bhakti, q.v., viz. śānti, dāsya, sākhya, vātsalya, and mādhurya), W.
