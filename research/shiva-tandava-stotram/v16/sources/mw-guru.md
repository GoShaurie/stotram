[p. 359,2] guru/ : mf(vī)n. (cf. giri/; comp. ga/rīyas, once ˚yas-tara, guru-tara, superl. gariṣṭha, gurutama See ss.vv.) heavy, weighty (opposed to laghu/), RV. i, 39, 3 and iv, 5, 6; AV. &c. (g. śauṇḍādi, Gaṇar. 101)

[p. 359,2] : heavy in the stomach (food), difficult to digest, MBh. i, 3334; Suśr.

[p. 359,2] : great, large, extended, long, Yājñ. (see -kratu), Bhartṛ. &c.

[p. 359,2] : (in prosody) long by nature or position (a vowel), Prāt. (a vowel long both by nature and by position is called garīyas, RPrāt. xviii, 20), Pāṇ. i, 4, 11 and 12

[p. 359,2] : high in degree, vehement, violent, excessive, difficult, hard, RV.; MBh. &c.

[p. 359,2] : grievous, Megh. 80

[p. 359,2] : important, serious, momentous, MBh. &c.

[p. 359,2] : valuable, highly prized, Yājñ. ii, 30 (guru = garīyas) &c.

[p. 359,2] : haughty, proud (speech), Pañcat.

[p. 359,2] : venerable, respectable

[p. 359,2] guru/ : m. any venerable or respectable person (father, mother, or any relative older than one's self), Gobh.; ŚāṅkhGṛ.; Mn. &c.

[p. 359,2] : a spiritual parent or preceptor (from whom a youth receives the initiatory Mantra or prayer, who instructs him in the Śāstras and conducts the necessary ceremonies up to that of investiture which is performed by the Ācārya, Yājñ. i, 34), RPrāt.; ĀśvGṛ.; PārGṛ.; Mn. &c.

[p. 359,2] : the chief of (gen. or in comp.), Cāṇ.; Ragh. ii, 68

[p. 359,2] : (with Śāktas) author of a Mantra

[p. 359,2] : ‘preceptor of the gods’, Bṛhaspati, Mn. xi

[p. 359,2] : (hence) the planet Jupiter, Jyot.; VarBṛS.; Bhartṛ. &c.

[p. 359,2] : ‘Pāṇḍu-teacher’, Droṇa, L.

[p. 359,2] : Prabhā-kara (celebrated teacher of the Mīmāṃsā, usually mentioned with Kumārila), SŚaṃkar. vi, 50; xv, 157

[p. 359,2] : (= dharma) ‘venerable’, the 9th astrological mansion, VarBṛS. i, 16

[p. 359,2] : Mucuna pruritus, L.

[p. 359,2] : N. of a son of Saṃkṛti, BhP. ix, 21, 2 du. parents, MBh.

[p. 359,2] guru/ : m. pl. parents and other venerable persons, Mn. iv; Vikr. v, 10; Kathās.

[p. 359,2] : a honorific appellation of a preceptor (whose N. is also put in the pl.), Jain, Hit.
