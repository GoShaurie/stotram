[p. 893,2] lag : (cf. √ lakṣ, lakṣa &c.) cl. 1. P. (Dhātup. xix, 24) lagati (accord. to Nir. iv, 10 also lagyati; pf. lalāga Gr.; aor. alagīt, ib.; fut. lagitā, ib.; lagiṣyati, Pañcat.; ind.p. lagitvā, -lagya, Kāv.),
to adhere, stick, cling or attach one's self to (loc.), MBh.; Kāv. &c. (with hṛdi and gen., ‘to penetrate to a person's heart’, Kathās.);
to take effect upon (loc.), Śiś.;
to meet, come in contact, cut (as lines), Gol., Sch.;
to follow closely, ensue or happen immediately, Kathās.;
to pass away (as time), Pañcat. :
Caus. orCaus. lagayati, or cl. 10. (Dhātup. xxxiii, 63) lāgayati, ‘to taste’ or ‘to obtain’ (āsvādane, or āsādane). [In Hindī this root often means ‘to begin’.]
