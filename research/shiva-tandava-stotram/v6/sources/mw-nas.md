[p. 532,2] 1. nas : encl. form for acc. gen. dat. pl. of the 1st pers. pron. (Pāṇ. viii, 1, 21), us, of us, to us; in Veda changeable into ṇas (4, 27; 28).

[p. 532,2] nas, : [cf. Zd. na, our; Gk. νῶϊ, νώ; Lat. nos, nos-ter; Old Lat. dat. nis.]

[p. 532,2] 2. nas : cl. 1. Ā. (Dhātup. xvi, 26) na/sate (aor. Pot. nasīmahi, RV. ii, 16, 8; pf. nese; fut. nasitā Gr.)
to approach, resort to, join, copulate (esp. as husband and wife), RV.;
to be crooked or fraudulent, Dhātup.

[p. 532,2] nas : [cf. Gk.νέ(σ)ομαι, να(σ)ίω, νόσ-τος; Goth. ga-nizan, nas-jan; Angl.Sax. genësan; HGerm. ginësan, genesen.]

[p. 532,2] 3. na/s or nās : f. (the strong stem occurs only in du. nā/sā, RV. ii, 39, 6, the weak stem only in nasā/, nasi/, naso/s [cf. Pāṇ. vi, 1, 63] and in comp.) the nose, RV.; AV.; VS.; TS.; BhP.

[p. 532,2] nas : [cf. nāsā, nāsikā; Lat. nas-turcium, nāres; Lith. nósis; Slav. nosǔ; Germ. Nase; Angl.Sax. nosu; Eng. nose, nostril = nose-thrill, nose-hole.]
