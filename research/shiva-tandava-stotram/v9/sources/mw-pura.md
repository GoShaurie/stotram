[p. 634,2] 1. pura : (for 2. See p. 635), in comp. for puras.

[p. 635,2] 2. pura : n. (for 1. See p. 634, col. 2) (ifc. f(ā). ) a fortress, castle, city, town (a place containing large buildings surrounded by a ditch and extending not less than one Kos in length ; if it extends for half that distance it is called a kheṭa, if less than that, a karvaṭa or small market town; any smaller cluster of houses is called a grāma or village, W.), Mn.; MBh. &c.

[p. 635,3] : the female apartments, gynaeceum, MBh. (cf. antaḥ-p˚, nārī-p˚ &c.)

[p. 635,3] : a house, abode, residence, receptacle, BhP.; Tattvas.

[p. 635,3] : an upper story, L.

[p. 635,3] : a brothel, L.

[p. 635,3] : ‘the city’ Gk. κατ᾽ ἐξοχήν i.e. Pāṭali-putra or Patnā, L.

[p. 635,3] : = tri-pura, the 3 strongholds of the Asuras, Kathās.

[p. 635,3] : the body (cf. 3. pur), BhP.

[p. 635,3] : the skin, L.

[p. 635,3] : a species of Cyperus, L.

[p. 635,3] : N. of a constellation, Var.

[p. 635,3] : a leaf rolled into the shape of a funnel, L. (prob. w.r. for puṭa)

[p. 635,3] : N. of the subdivisions of the Vedānta wk. tripurī or tripuṭī (perhaps also w.r. for puṭa), Cat.

[p. 635,3] pura : mf(ā)n. a kind of resin, bdellium, Suśr.; L.

[p. 635,3] pura : m. N. of an Asura = tri-pura (cf. pura-jit), of another man, g. kurv-ādi
