[p. 532,3] nāga/ : m. (prob. neither fr. na-ga nor fr. nagna) a snake, (esp.) Coluber Naga, ŚBr.; MBh. &c.

[p. 532,3] : (f. ī/, Suparṇ.) a Nāga or serpent-demon (the race of Kadru or Su-rasā inhabiting the waters or the city Bhoga-vatī under the earth; they are supposed to have a human face with serpent-like lower extremities [see esp. Nāg. v, 17; RTL. 233 &c.]; their kings are Śeṣa, Vāsuki, and Takṣaka, ib. 323; 7 or 8 of the Nāgas are particularly mentioned, MBh.; Kāv. &c.; with Buddhists they are also represented as ordinary men, MWB. 220)

[p. 533,1] : N. of the numbers 7 (Sūryas.) or 8 (Hcat.)

[p. 533,1] : a cruel man, L.

[p. 533,1] : one of the 5 airs of the human body (which is expelled by eructation), Vedāntas.

[p. 533,1] : (sg. also collect.) an elephant (f(ī). , ifc. f(ā). ), Kāv.; BhP. etc.

[p. 533,1] : the best or most excellent of any kind (ifc.), L. (cf. ṛṣabha, vyāghra &c.)

[p. 533,1] : shark, L.
