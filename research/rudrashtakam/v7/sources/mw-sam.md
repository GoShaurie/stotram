[p. 1152,1] 1. sam or stam : cl. 1. P. samati or stamati, to be disturbed (accord. to some ‘to be undisturbed’; cf. √ śam), Dhātup. xix, 82;
 cl. 10. P. samayati or stamayati, to be agitated or disturbed, Vop.

[p. 1152,1] 2. sa/m : ind. (connected with 7. sa and 2. sama, and opp. to 3. vi, q.v.) with, together with, along with, together, altogether (used as a preposition or prefix to verbs and verbal derivatives, like Gk. σύν, Lat. con, and expressing ‘conjunction’, ‘union’, ‘thoroughness’, ‘intensity’, ‘completeness’ e.g. saṃ√ yuj, ‘to join together’;
saṃ-√ dhā, ‘to place together’;
saṃ-dhi, ‘placing together’;
saṃ-√ tap, ‘to consume utterly by burning’;
sam-uccheda, ‘destroying altogether, complete destruction’;
in Ved. the verb connected with it has sometimes to be supplied, e.g. ā/po agni/m yaśa/saḥ sa/ṃ hi/ pūrvī/ḥ, ‘for many glorious waters surrounded Agni’;
it is sometimes prefixed to nouns in the sense of 2. sama, ‘same’;
cf. samartha), RV. &c.;
