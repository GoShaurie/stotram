[p. 617,1] pāda : m. (ifc. f(ā). , rarely f(ī). ) the foot (of men and animals), RV. &c. &c. (the pl. sometimes added to proper names or titles in token of respect, e.g. deva-pādāḥ, ‘the king's majesty’, Pañc. ; nārāyaṇap˚, ‘the venerable N˚’, Sāh.; pādaiḥ ind. on foot [said of several persons], R.; ˚dayoḥ and ˚de-√ pat, to fall at a person's [gen.] feet, Kāv.; Hit.)

[p. 617,2] : the foot or leg of an inanimate object, column, pillar, AV.; ŚBr.; MBh. &c.

[p. 617,2] : a wheel, Śiś. xii, 21

[p. 617,2] : a foot as a measure (= 12 Aṅgulas), ŚBr.; ŚrS.; MārkP.

[p. 617,2] : the foot or root of a tree, Var.

[p. 617,2] : the foot or a hill at the f˚ of a mountain, MBh.; Kāv. &c.

[p. 617,2] : the bottom (dṛteh pādāt, ‘from the b˚ of a bag’ v.l. pātrāt), MBh. v, 1047

[p. 617,2] : a ray or beam of light (considered as the f˚ of a heavenly body), ib.

[p. 617,2] : a quarter, a fourth part (the f˚ of a quadruped being one out of 4), ŚBr.; Mn.; MBh. &c. (pl. the 4 parts i.e. all things required for [gen.] Suśr.)

[p. 617,2] : the quadrant (of a circle), Āryabh., Sch.

[p. 617,2] : a verse or line (as the fourth part of a regular stanza), Br.; ŚrS.; Prāt. &c.

[p. 617,2] : the caesura of a verse, AgP., the chapter of a book (orig. only of a book or section of a b˚ consisting of 4 parts, as the Adhyāyas of Pāṇini's grammar).
