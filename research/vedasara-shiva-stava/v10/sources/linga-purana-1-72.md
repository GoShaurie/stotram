[J. L. Shastri translation, pp. 349–351 (OCR; diacritics garbled by OCR are left as printed)]

33. Then he drove the horses that had the speed of the wind, towards the cities of the swift and courageous Danavas. The cities till then had peace and comfort.
34. Thereafter looking at Devas, lord Rudra said; — "Give unto me the lordship of Pasus. Then I shall kill the Asuras.
35. O excellent Devas, only after assigning you a status of animal souls distinct from that of Devas and others, can they be killed, not otherwise, O excellent ones."
36. After hearing these words of the intelligent lord, all of them became suspicious and uneasy over this change and felt great distress.
37. On realising their mental reaction, the lord spoke to them thus: — "O excellent Devas, let there be no fear or misgiving in you in regard to your becoming Pai§us.
38-41. Now listen and try to pursue the means of liberation from the state of Pasu. He who performs the divine holy rite "Pasupata" will be liberated from the state of Pas u. O pure ones, I solemnly promise this to you. O excellent Devas, there is no doubt in this that those others too who perform the Pasupata rite will be liberated from the state of a Pa^u. He who renders service steadily for twelve years or even half that period or even three years can be liberated from that state. Hence, O excellent Devas, perform this great and divine rite".
42-50. "So be it", said Devas to Siva, who is bowed to by all the worlds. That is why Devas, Asuras and human beings are called Pas us. Rudra is the lord of Pa^us and the liberator of Pa^us from their bondage. He who is Pasu, shall eschew that state through this holy rite. [...]

Shastri's footnotes 628–629 (p. 350): "628. paSunam adhipatyam — lordship of the individual souls (jivas) [...]" / "629. Siva is named Pasupati, the lord of animals. According to the legend, recorded in the present chapter, every deity was asked by Siva to declare himself a mere Paiu or animal before Tripuras could be slain in the battle. [...] The lord then enjoined the observance of Pasupata vrata for the attainment of their release from animal nature."
