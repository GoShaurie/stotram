[p. 172,2] ugra/ : mfn. (said to be fr. √ uc [Uṇ. ii, 29], but probably fr. a √ uj, or √ vaj, fr. which also ojas, vāja, vajra may be derived; compar. ugratara and o/jīyas; superl. ugratama and o/jiṣṭha), powerful, violent, mighty, impetuous, strong, huge, formidable, terrible

[p. 172,2] : high, noble

[p. 172,2] : cruel, fierce, ferocious, savage

[p. 172,2] : angry, passionate, wrathful

[p. 172,2] : hot, sharp, pungent, acrid, RV.; AV.; TS.; R.; Śak.; Ragh. &c.

[p. 172,2] ugra/ : m. N. of Rudra or Śiva, MBh.; VP.

[p. 172,2] : of a particular Rudra, BhP.

[p. 172,2] : N. of a mixed tribe (from a Kṣatriya father and Śūdra mother; the Ugra, according to Manu. x, 9, is of cruel or rude [krūra] conduct [ācāra] and employment [vihāra], as killing or catching snakes &c.; but according to the Tantras he is an encomiast or bard), Mn.; Yājñ. &c.

[p. 172,2] : a twice-born man who perpetrates dreadful deeds Comm. on Āp. i, 7, 20; Āp.; Gaut.

[p. 172,2] : the tree Hyperanthera Moringa, L.

[p. 172,2] : N. of a Dānava, Hariv.

[p. 172,2] : a son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, MBh.

[p. 172,2] : the Guru of Narendrāditya (who built a temple called Ugreśa)

[p. 172,2] : a group of asterisms (viz. pūrva-phālgunī, pūrvāṣāḍhā, pūrva-bhādrapadā, maghā, bharaṇī)

[p. 172,2] : N. of the Malabar country

[p. 172,2] ugra/ : (am), n. a particular poison, the root of Aconitum Ferox

[p. 172,2] : wrath, anger;

[p. 172,2] ugra, : [cf. Zd. ughra: Gk. ὑγι-ής, ὑγίεια, Lat. augeo &c. : Goth. auka, ‘I increase’; Lith. ug-is, ‘growth, increase’; aug-u, ‘I grow’, &c.]
